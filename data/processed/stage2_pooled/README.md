# Pooled fine-tuning (all 5 domains combined) -- result: worse, not better

You asked to fine-tune on more of the real experimental datasets together
to try for a higher R2. Ran it for both architectures. Honest result:
**pooling made accuracy worse than separate per-domain fine-tuning (Stage
2) in 8 of 10 domain x architecture combinations**, one of them
catastrophically.

## Setup
All 5 real fine-tuning domains (hardik2016, hardik2017, kaeri uniform,
kaeri nonuniform, zhao2020) concatenated into one 3,189-row set (2,077
train / 476 val / 636 test, stratified per-domain so no domain is starved
out of any split), then fine-tuned as ONE shared model per architecture
(starting from the same Stage 1 pretrained checkpoint used in Stage 2).

## Results: Stage 2 (separate per-domain) vs. pooled

| Domain | MLP separate | MLP pooled | Transformer separate | Transformer pooled |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| hardik2016 coils (R123) | 0.562 | 0.143 | 0.748 | 0.647 |
| hardik2017 straight tubes (R123) | 0.481 | **-27.90** | -0.053 | -0.238 |
| kaeri uniform (water) | 0.907 | 0.736 | 0.905 | 0.857 |
| kaeri nonuniform (water) | 0.867 | 0.762 | 0.837 | **0.861** |
| zhao2020 tubes (water) | 0.890 | 0.696 | 0.892 | 0.799 |

Only one cell improved (Transformer, kaeri_nonuniform, +0.024) -- everything
else got worse, one catastrophically (MLP on hardik2017: R2 = -27.9, wildly
broken).

## Why
The domains are badly imbalanced (kaeri+zhao2020 water rows heavily
outnumber the R123 rows: ~1,939 vs. ~138 combined train rows) and represent
genuinely different fluid/geometry regimes. A single shared fine-tune
optimizes for the majority water domains and actively hurts the minority
R123 ones -- most visibly for the MLP on hardik2017 (only 36 training rows
in the pooled set, swamped by ~1,939 water rows). The Transformer degrades
less severely than the MLP under pooling (no comparable blow-up), which
may reflect self-attention handling heterogeneous inputs somewhat more
gracefully than a plain MLP, though neither model was given an explicit
domain/fluid indicator that might have helped both.

## Conclusion
Separate per-domain fine-tuning (Stage 2's original approach) is the
better strategy for these datasets as they stand. Pooling more data only
helps when domains are compatible enough to share one decision boundary --
these aren't, primarily because of the fluid split (R123 vs. water) and
the severe row-count imbalance.
